## Active Threats & Incidents

### CaptiveCrunch: Midnight Blizzard hijacks hotel/guest Wi-Fi to deliver CornFlake RAT
**Source:** [Microsoft Security Blog (Tier 1)](https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/security/blog/2026/07/31/captivecrunch-midnight-blizzard-targets-travelers-worldwide-for-malware-delivery-and-credential-theft/) · **Published:** July 31, 2026

Microsoft disclosed a campaign, tracked as CaptiveCrunch, in which threat actors compromise hotel and other guest Wi-Fi captive portals to serve a fake browser-update prompt that installs CornFlake, a remote access trojan capable of capturing webcam images, microphone audio, and keystrokes. Microsoft attributes the activity to Storm-2945, assessed as an operational sub-cluster of Midnight Blizzard (also tracked as APT29/Cozy Bear), and says the campaign has been running since early May 2026, targeting travelers on hospitality and other guest networks worldwide. This item was not captured in the 2026-07-31 digest (likely published after that run completed); also picked up by [The Hacker News (Tier 2)](https://thehackernews.com/2026/08/hijacked-hotel-wi-fi-pushes-fake.html) on August 1, 2026.

### Adform ad-tech script trojanized to hijack cryptocurrency clipboard contents
**Source:** [BleepingComputer (Tier 1)](https://www.bleepingcomputer.com/news/security/online-ad-firm-adforms-script-compromised-to-steal-cryptocurrency/) · **Published:** August 1, 2026

Attackers modified `trackpoint-async.js`, a JavaScript tracking library served by ad-tech firm Adform from `s2.adform.net` and embedded on sites using its platform (Adform serves roughly 14,000 customers with about 30% share of the demand-side ad category). The trojanized script continuously monitored visitors' clipboards and, when it detected a Bitcoin, Ethereum, or TRON wallet address, replaced it with an attacker-controlled address to redirect cryptocurrency payments; it could also rewrite wallet addresses displayed directly on web pages. Adform says it detected the incident on July 27, 2026, removed the malicious code, notified affected clients, and reported it to authorities; security researcher Kevin Beaumont (DoublePulsar) first surfaced the compromise. Also reported by [The Hacker News (Tier 2)](https://thehackernews.com/2026/08/hackers-poison-adform-script-to-swap.html), August 1, 2026.
